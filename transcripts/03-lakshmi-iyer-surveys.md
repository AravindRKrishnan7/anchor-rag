title: What a hull survey really finds
date: 2026-10-03

Interviewer: What kind of surveys do you do most often?
Lakshmi Iyer: Condition surveys for insurers and pre-purchase inspections for owners looking at second-hand tonnage. Insurers want to know the risk they are taking on. A purchaser wants to know what the seller is not telling them.

Interviewer: What is the first thing you look for on an older ship?
Lakshmi Iyer: The ballast tanks. Seawater sits in them for years, and once the coating starts to break down, corrosion spreads fast and out of sight. I have seen tanks where the plating had lost a third of its thickness while the deck above looked freshly painted.

Interviewer: How can a purchaser protect themselves?
Lakshmi Iyer: Insist on entering the tanks, not just reading the class records. Ask for the thickness measurement reports and check when they were taken. If the seller keeps the ship busy so there is no time for an inspection, that alone tells you something.

Interviewer: Have you ever found something that stopped a sale?
Lakshmi Iyer: Yes, a bulk carrier with cracked side-shell frames in the forward holds, hidden behind cargo residue. The repairs would have cost more than the discount the buyer had negotiated, so they walked away.

Interviewer: What has changed in your work over the last few years?
Lakshmi Iyer: Drones and remote cameras let us look inside large tanks without staging, which is safer and quicker. But a camera cannot tap the steel or smell a wet void space. I still go in myself for anything that matters.
