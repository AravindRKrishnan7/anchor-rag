title: Life in the engine room
date: 2026-09-14

Interviewer: What does a normal day look like for a chief engineer?
Ravi Menon: It starts with the morning round. I walk the engine room with the duty engineer, read the logbook, check temperatures and pressures, and listen. After twenty years you can hear a fuel pump that is unhappy before any alarm goes off.

Interviewer: What is the worst thing that has happened to you at sea?
Ravi Menon: A complete blackout in the Bay of Bengal during the monsoon. Both generators tripped within a minute of each other, the main engine stopped, and the ship was drifting beam-on to a heavy swell. We had the emergency generator online quickly, but it took almost forty minutes to restart the main engine. Nobody slept that night.

Interviewer: What caused it?
Ravi Menon: Contaminated fuel. A bad bunker delivery clogged the separators and the purifiers could not keep up. Since then I insist on sending a sample to the lab before we burn anything from a new supplier, even if the charterer complains about the delay.

Interviewer: How do you stop breakdowns before they happen?
Ravi Menon: Planned maintenance, done honestly. Every pump, compressor and cylinder unit has running hours and a schedule. The trap is signing off jobs on paper that were never done. I would rather report an overdue job to the office than pretend it is finished.

Interviewer: What advice would you give a young engineer?
Ravi Menon: Learn the systems with your hands, not only from the manual. Trace the pipes yourself, open the equipment during overhauls, and ask the fitter why he does things a certain way. The people who panic in an emergency are usually the ones who never got their hands dirty.
